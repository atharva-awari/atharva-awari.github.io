I design optimization algorithms for low-rank models that go beyond linearity.

Classical factorizations write data as X ≈ WH. My work studies the nonlinear version X ≈ f(WH), and its tensor counterpart, which describes sparse, binary, bounded or clipped data with far fewer factors. I build these models into a single ADMM framework and release the code openly.

ResearchFrom my thesis, Beyond Linearity: Nonlinear Matrix and Tensor Decompositions

Sparse data and the ReLU

X ≈ max(0, WH)

A sparse matrix can have high rank and still be the ReLU of a matrix of very low rank: the zeros only ask for nonpositive entries, and that freedom lowers the rank. The identity matrix of any size is the ReLU of a rank-3 matrix.

On a dictionary of sparse face features, a rank-20 ReLU decomposition reaches 3% error where the best linear rank-20 approximation stays at 78%. I developed accelerated algorithms for this model (A-NMD, 3B-NMD) and the first coordinate descent method that solves the original problem directly.

MLSP 2023, EUSIPCO 2024

Binary and bounded data

X ≈ σ(WH)

With the sigmoid, the model fits data with entries in [0, 1] or of a binary nature, such as presence/absence patterns, ratings or adjacency matrices. A block coordinate descent method with a scaling step and a TSVD-based initialization compresses binary data sets and completes missing entries better than the truncated SVD and ReLU-NMD.

MLSP 2025, with H. Nguyen

One algorithm for many models

minW,H d(X, f(WH))

Splitting T = WH gives an ADMM scheme in which only one entrywise update depends on the nonlinearity f and the loss d. Four nonlinearities (ReLU, square, MinMax, modulus) and three losses (Frobenius, ℓ1, Kullback–Leibler) give twelve models, with missing data handled for free.

Matching the loss to the noise matters: under Poisson noise, MinMax with the KL divergence recovers the image with 9.9% error, against 19.9% for the Frobenius norm. For ReLU-NMD the same framework beats coordinate descent and a tuned Adam baseline.

Under review, arXiv:2512.17473

From matrices to tensors

𝒳 ≈ f(⟦A, B, C⟧)

The same splitting carries over to the canonical polyadic decomposition: a global ADMM scheme fits CPD under the Frobenius, ℓ1 and KL losses, succeeds more often than AO-ADMM on synthetic tensors, and denoises the Indian Pines hyperspectral cube (307 × 307 × 162) under Poisson noise with 0.7% error.

With a clipping nonlinearity, the model handles values censored at an instrument's limit of detection, as in NMR metabolomics, and recovers the underlying patterns more faithfully than treating those entries as missing or imputing them.

EUSIPCO 2026; arXiv:2610.05339 (ICASSP 2027 submission)

Thesis

  • Beyond linearity: nonlinear matrix and tensor decompositions A. Awari, supervised by N. Gillis and A. Vandaele PhD thesis, University of Mons, 2026

SoftwareCode for every paper, with the experiments

  • ADMM for Nonlinear CP Decompositions

    ADMM for nonlinear CP decompositions, where an element-wise nonlinearity models clipped or censored data, such as values below a limit of detection in metabolomics or saturated audio recordings.

  • ADMM for NMDsMATLAB

    ADMM for twelve nonlinear matrix decompositions (ReLU, square, MinMax, modulus with Frobenius, ℓ1 or KL), with missing data and an adaptive penalty.

  • ADMM for Generalized Tensor DecompositionsPython

    Global ADMM for the canonical polyadic decomposition under Frobenius, ℓ1 and KL losses, with the AO-ADMM comparison.

  • Coordinate Descent for ReLU-NMDMATLAB, Julia, C++

    Exact coordinate descent for ReLU-NMD, solving each scalar subproblem by sweeping its breakpoints; multithreaded.

  • Accelerated ReLU-NMDMATLAB

    A-NMD and 3B-NMD with nuclear-norm initialization, with N. Gillis and G. Seraghiti.

  • Sigmoid NMDPython

    Block coordinate descent for the sigmoid decomposition, with H. Nguyen.
